The festival

A living dialogue between two temple cultures

Rooted in temple culture, oral traditions, and embodied learning systems, Bali in Bengaluru creates a space for artistic exchange, learning, and public engagement. Over fifteen days, master artists from Bali and India share the stage, the workshop, and the classroom.

  • Duration 15 days
  • Venues Auditoriums, performance spaces, universities
  • Format Workshops, lectures, performances

Balinese culture & its Bhāratīya roots

Shared stories, reinterpreted

Balinese culture is a living testament to the deep historical and spiritual connections between the island of Bali and the traditions of Bhārat. Hinduism remains its dominant spiritual framework, and the great epics — the Ramayana and Mahabharata — are central to it.

These epics are core narrative sources for rituals, temple ceremonies, visual arts, dance dramas, shadow puppetry (Wayang Kulit), and seasonal performances across the island — continually reinterpreted and localised in Balinese aesthetics, theology, and practice.

Featuring

Dr. I Wayan Dibia

Padma Shri awardee & team

Dr. I Wayan Dibia is a renowned Balinese artist, scholar, and choreographer known for bridging Balinese performing arts with Bhāratīya epic traditions. Born in Singapadu, Bali, into a family of artists, he trained in dance and music from a young age and later pursued advanced studies in Southeast Asian performing arts, including postgraduate work in the United States.

Over his career he has authored several books and choreographed more than 150 works inspired by the Ramayana and Mahabharata. In 2021 he was awarded the Padma Shri by the Government of India for his contributions to the arts and cross-cultural dialogue.

Festival events

Fifteen days of making, learning & performing

Workshops

2 Days

Shadow Puppetry Workshop

An introduction to Balinese and Indian shadow traditions — techniques of light, material, movement, and narration. Participants develop short sequences, and selected performers appear alongside master artists in a live presentation.

2 Days

Mask-Making Workshop

Exploring Balinese and Indian mask traditions — symbolism, facial geometry, colour, and expression. Hands-on creation of masks using traditional and accessible materials, exhibited as part of the festival display.

Academic interactions

Dance & Movement Classes

Traditional Balinese dance techniques, with a focus on body discipline, expression, and musicality.

Lecture–Demonstration Sessions

An introduction to Balinese arts and culture by Dr. Wayan Dibia for university students and faculty, practitioners, researchers, scholars, and heritage enthusiasts across Bengaluru.

Performances

Shadow Puppetry Collaboration

Indonesian and Indian shadow puppets and stories brought together by master artists — a common thread of Ramayana and Mahabharata.

Balinese Dance × Yakshagana

The vocal rhythm and choral structures of Balinese dance meet the percussive, movement-based vocabulary of Yakshagana — bound by the Ramayana.

Kecak Performance

The famed “Ramayana Monkey Chant.” A powerful “cak-cak-cak” chorus of seated men builds rhythm and atmosphere without instruments while dancers perform in classical Balinese style.

Param Foundation

Param Foundation is a transdisciplinary ecosystem designed to inspire creativity, empower innovation, and celebrate India’s rich cultural, historical, and scientific heritage. Through a network of physical centres and digital platforms, it brings together art, science, history, culture, and technology to transform lives and build communities.

Param Culture is dedicated to encouraging, organising, and elevating the essence of Bhāratīya culture — connecting practitioners and academicians through research, immersive experiences, storytelling, and innovation, towards a sustainable, interconnected, and globally relevant culture ecosystem.
